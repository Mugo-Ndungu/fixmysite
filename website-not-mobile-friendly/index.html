Website doesn't work properly on phones?

Most visitors to small business websites are on phones. If yours shows a tiny desktop page, has text you must zoom to read, or buttons too small to tap, people leave. Older sites often just lack one line of code; others need a responsive theme.

1. Check it like a customer

Open your site on your own phone, without zooming. Can you read the text, tap the phone number, and find opening hours and the booking button? On a computer, Chrome's developer tools have a device mode that shows the same thing.

2. The missing viewport tag

View the page source and search for viewport. If there's no <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"> line, phones show the desktop page shrunk down. Adding it is the first step, though the layout may then need adjusting.

3. Fixed-width layouts

Sites built years ago often use fixed widths, such as a 960-pixel page. They need flexible widths and images that scale down. On WordPress, Wix or Squarespace, switching to a modern responsive theme or template solves most of it.

4. Text, buttons and phone numbers

Body text should be at least 16 pixels. Buttons and links need room to tap. Make your phone number a tap-to-call link.

5. Pop-ups that cover the page

Full-screen pop-ups and cookie banners that can't be closed on a phone stop people dead. Make sure each one has a visible close button.
