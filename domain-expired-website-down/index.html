Guides: Domains, DNS and email

Domain expired and your website (and email) stopped working?

When a domain expires, the website and any email on it stop working. Most registrars let the owner renew at the normal price for a short time after expiry, then charge a much higher restore fee, and eventually release the domain for anyone to buy. Act today.

1. Confirm the domain has expired

Look your domain up at lookup.icann.org. It shows the expiry date and which company the domain is registered with (the registrar), such as GoDaddy, Namecheap or Squarespace.

2. Log in to the registrar and renew

Renew from the domain's page in your account. If it expired recently, renewal is usually at the normal price. The website and email come back once the renewal goes through, sometimes within minutes, sometimes a few hours.

3. If you can't get into the registrar account

This is common when a former web designer or employee registered the domain. Contact the registrar's support straight away and ask how to prove you own the business. Keep proof ready: business registration, invoices, and the domain's old contact details if you know them.

4. If renewal now costs a lot more

After the first grace period, registrars move domains into a "redemption" stage with a restore fee. It's often still worth paying: a new domain means new business cards, lost Google rankings, and missing emails.

5. Make sure it never happens again

  • Turn on auto-renew and renew for several years at once.
  • Make sure the registrar's contact email is one you read, not an old employee's.
  • Keep a card on file that won't expire soon.
