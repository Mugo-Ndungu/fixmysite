Guides: Domains, DNS and email

Email on your business domain stopped working?

When email on your own domain stops, it's usually one of three things: the domain expired, the email subscription (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or your host's email) lapsed, or the domain's email records were changed during a website move.

1. Check the domain hasn't expired

If the website is down too, look the domain up at lookup.icann.org. An expired domain stops both. See the expired domain guide.

2. Check the email subscription

Log in to your email provider's admin console and check billing. A failed card payment can suspend every mailbox.

3. Check the MX records

Your domain's MX records tell the world where to deliver your email. If someone changed name servers or DNS during a website move, the MX records may have been lost. Your email provider lists exactly what they should be. Compare and fix them where your DNS is controlled (see the DNS guide).

4. Read the bounce message

Ask someone to email you and forward the bounce. "Mailbox full", "user unknown" or "domain not found" each point to a different fix.

5. Emails arrive but yours go to spam

Your domain needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC records. Your email provider gives you the exact values to add.
