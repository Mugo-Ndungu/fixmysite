Guides: Security warnings and hacks

Google shows "This site may be hacked" or Chrome says "Dangerous site"?

Google adds these warnings when it finds hacked content, spam pages or malware on your site. Customers see them before they even reach you. The site has to be cleaned, then you ask Google to review it in Search Console, and the warning usually goes within days.

1. See what Google found

Add the site to Google Search Console and open Security issues. It lists the problem type and example pages. Google's Safe Browsing site status page also shows whether your address is flagged.

2. Look for spam pages

Search Google for site:yourbusiness.com. Pages you never made (cheap pills, casinos, pages in other languages) are a sign of a hack that creates spam pages.

3. Clean the site

Back up first, change every password, update everything, and remove the malicious files and spam pages. On WordPress, follow the steps in the hacked WordPress guide. Restoring a clean backup from before the hack is often fastest.

4. Ask Google for a review

In Security issues, tick that you've fixed the problems and click Request review. Describe what you found and removed. Malware reviews are often done within a few days; spam reviews can take longer.

5. Remove leftover spam pages from Google

Make sure the spam addresses now return "not found", and use Search Console's Removals tool to hide them from results faster.
