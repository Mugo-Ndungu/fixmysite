Browser says your website is "Not secure"?

The warning appears when your site's SSL certificate is missing, has expired, or was issued for a different address (for example www but not the plain domain). Most hosts give you a free certificate that renews itself. It just has to be issued correctly and cover every version of your address.

1. Find out which problem you have

In Chrome, click the warning next to your address, then the certificate details. Look at two things: the valid until date, and the name it was issued to. An old date means it expired. A different name means it belongs to another address, often your hosting company's server.

2. If it expired

  • cPanel hosting (common with many US hosts): open SSL/TLS Status and click Run AutoSSL.
  • Other hosts: look for "SSL" in your control panel. Most offer a free Let's Encrypt certificate that renews automatically.
  • If a certificate was installed by hand years ago, it won't renew on its own. Replace it with an automatic one.

3. If it's for the wrong address

Make sure the certificate covers both yourbusiness.com and www.yourbusiness.com, or redirect one to the other. This is the most common cause of a warning that only some visitors see.

4. Wix, Squarespace and Shopify

These builders issue the certificate for you once your domain is connected properly. A warning on one of them usually means the domain's DNS settings point somewhere else.

5. Send everyone to the secure address

Turn on "Force HTTPS" (or redirect http to https) so nobody lands on the insecure version. If the padlock still shows a warning with a valid certificate, some images or scripts are loading over plain http. They need updating to https.
