Moved to a new website but your domain still shows the old one (or nothing)?

Your domain's DNS settings decide where visitors go. After a move, they often still point to the old host, point to both, or only cover one of www or the plain domain. Changes can take up to 48 hours to reach everyone.

1. Find out who controls your DNS

Look up your domain at lookup.icann.org and note the name servers. Those decide where you edit DNS. It may be your registrar, your old host, or a service such as Cloudflare.

2. Enter exactly what the new host asks for

Your new host or builder gives you either new name servers or specific records: usually an A record for the plain domain and a CNAME for www. Enter them where your DNS is controlled.

3. Remove the old records

A common mistake: leaving the old A record in place next to the new one. Visitors then land on the old site or the new one at random. Delete records that point to the old host.

4. Cover both www and the plain domain

Test yourbusiness.com and www.yourbusiness.com. Both should reach the new site, with one redirecting to the other, and both with the padlock.

5. Don't break your email

If you change name servers, your email records (MX, plus SPF and DKIM) must be recreated at the new DNS provider, or email stops. Copy them before switching.

6. Give it time, then test from outside

Allow a few hours, sometimes up to 48. A public DNS checker website shows what different parts of the world see, which is more reliable than your own browser's cache.
